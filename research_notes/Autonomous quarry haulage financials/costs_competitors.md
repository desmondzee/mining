# Cost of Haul-Truck Autonomy (OEM vs Retrofit) and Competitive Landscape with Funding

Research date: 2026-10-17. Years are flagged on every number. "EST" = estimate (mine or a third party's); "VENDOR" = company-reported claim. Reseller/marketplace prices are not official list prices.

## 1. Cost of OEM autonomous haulage (Cat Command, Komatsu FrontRunner): per truck, per site, minimum fleet

### Takeaway
Neither Caterpillar nor Komatsu publishes AHS prices. The best public anchor is Boliden Aitik in 2021: SEK 218M (~US$25M) to automate 11 Komatsu 930E trucks, roughly $2.3M per truck all-in (my EST). A 2023 consultant model assumes $1.5M per truck to fit AHS. Third-party guides put retrofits at $0.8–1.5M per ultra-class truck, plus $3–15M per site for infrastructure. Mining OEM AHS is built for 100 t+ trucks and large fleets. Caterpillar only demonstrated a 100-ton-class Cat 777 at a quarry in Nov 2024 and says that offer is not yet commercial for aggregates.

### Cited Findings
- **Boliden Aitik (Sweden), 2021:** Komatsu FrontRunner AHS on 11 Komatsu 930E-5 ultra-class trucks. Total investment SEK 218M (~US$25M), fully implemented during 2024, including staff training. — [International Mining, 2021-10-22](https://im-mining.com/2021/10/22/boliden-to-become-eu-automation-frontrunner-with-help-of-komatsu-ahs/)
  - EST (mine): about US$2.3M per truck all-in, covering kit, infrastructure, integration and training.
- **Komatsu VENDOR claims (2020):** AHS reduced operating cost by an estimated $0.50–0.62 per ton and load-and-haul unit costs by up to 15%. Komatsu says customers may have saved up to $600M. — [Komatsu blog, 2020](https://www.komatsu.com/en-us/blog/2020/autonomous-mining-systems-offer-advantages)
- **Whittle Consulting / Pronto AHS financial model (~2023, vendor-sponsored):**
  - Inputs: truck $5.5M, plus $1.5M to adapt a truck to AHS; operator $220k per year.
  - AHS staffing: 4 people per shift for the whole fleet (2 in the control room, 2 field technicians), versus 1 operator per truck per shift when manned.
  - Result: autonomous 40 t trucks gave 31% higher NPV than manned 100 t trucks in a hypothetical 18-year copper mine. NPVs: autonomous small $510M; autonomous medium $479M; manned medium $390M; manned small $356M.
  - — [Whittle Consulting AHS Financial Model Assessment, Rev F (2023)](https://www.whittleconsulting.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Autonomous-Haulage-Study-Report-Rev-F.pdf)
- **Third-party guide, unsourced, treat as rough EST (2026):**
  - Retrofitting a 240–300 t haul truck: US$0.8–1.5M per truck.
  - New ultra-class autonomous truck: $5.5–9M per vehicle.
  - Site infrastructure: $3–15M per site.
  - Payback: about 3–6 years in high-labour regions.
  - Smaller operations (under 20 trucks) are "harder to justify."
  - — [DirtMatch 2026 guide](https://dirtmatch.com/blog/autonomous-semi-autonomous-haul-trucks-guide-2026)
- **Cat retrofit kits exist but have no public price.** Retrofit kits extend Command for hauling to existing Cat trucks and to other brands of trucks and loading equipment. Some new models ship "Command-ready" with part of the kit fitted at the factory. Cat VENDOR claim: up to 30% productivity gain. — [Cat press release (5 Bt milestone)](https://www.cat.com/en_US/news/machine-press-releases/milestone-caterpillar-surpasses-5-billion-tonnes-of-material-autonomously-hauled.html); [CEG (3 Bt milestone)](https://www.constructionequipmentguide.com/caterpillar-surpasses-3b-tonnes-hauled-autonomously-by-cat-command-for-hauling-trucks/52113)
- **Freeport-McMoRan:** converting 33 trucks to autonomous at one mine, an example of a mine-scale fleet retrofit. — [Construction Equipment](https://www.constructionequipment.com/earthmoving/news/33014251/freeport-mcmoran-mine-to-convert-33-trucks-to-autonomous)
- **Cat in quarries (Nov 2024):** fully autonomous Cat 777 (100-ton class) demonstrated at Luck Stone Bull Run, Virginia. It is Cat's first autonomy in aggregates, following a Dec 2022 Luck Stone agreement. Cat says testing and validation continue "before making autonomous technology commercially available in the aggregates industry." Cat fleet-wide: more than 8.62 Bt hauled autonomously and more than 325M km driven. — [Caterpillar press release, 2024-11-07](https://www.caterpillar.com/en/news/corporate-press-releases/h/autonomous-truck-luck-stone.html); [Caterpillar investor release](https://investors.caterpillar.com/news/news-details/2024/Caterpillar-Paves-the-Way-for-Future-Technology-Advancements-with-Launch-of-Autonomous-Cat-777-Off-Highway-Truck-at-Luck-Stone-Quarry/default.aspx)
- **Quarry truck sizes:** AHS was designed for trucks of 100 t and up. In quarries, 100 t is often the largest truck, and many use 25–70 t haulers. — [Pit & Quarry](https://www.pitandquarry.com/which-truck-size-is-best-for-autonomous-hauling/)
- **Komatsu scale (2026):** FrontRunner has passed 1,000 trucks. — [Geomechanics.io](https://www.geomechanics.io/news/article/komatsus-frontrunner-ahs-at-1000-trucks-haul-road-design-notes-for-engineers); [International Mining, 2026-09-22](https://im-mining.com/2026/09/22/komatsus-ahs-journey-past-present-and-future/)
- **Komatsu's quarry offer is Pronto's technology, not FrontRunner.** Komatsu Smart Quarry Autonomous is Pronto-based and sold through Komatsu dealers (Aug 2025). — [Pronto/Komatsu release](https://pronto.ai/komatsu-north-america-and-pronto-launch-autonomy-solution-for-quarry-size-trucks/); [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/komatsu-to-deploy-prontos-autonomous-haulage-technologies-to-quarry-operations/)

### Inferences
- **Total cost for OEM mine AHS:** EST about $1.5–2.5M per truck all-in, plus several $M per site. Fixed control-room and field staff (about 4 people per shift) penalise small fleets, which is why the industry heuristic is ~20+ trucks for OEM AHS.
- **Komatsu has no small-truck AHS of its own.** By reselling Pronto for quarries, it signals that FrontRunner economics do not scale down to quarry trucks. Cat's aggregates offer is still pre-commercial.
- **Room for a retrofit startup:** both points open space for a sub-$300k-per-truck retrofit with a subscription. That price target is my EST, not a sourced figure.

### Gaps
- No official Cat Command or FrontRunner price list, licence fee, or per-tonne licence model found. These are dealer and contract quotes only.
- No published minimum fleet size from Cat or Komatsu. The "under 20 trucks" threshold is from an unsourced guide.

## 2. Aftermarket / retrofit providers: pricing, business model, funding, customers

### Takeaway
Retrofit pricing is almost never public. The only public price points are dated:
- Built Robotics Exosystem: $3,000 per machine per month plus per-hour autonomy usage fees (2022).
- Carbon Robotics tractor kit: $15–25 per hour (agriculture).

Business models seen:
- Pronto/Atoms: no upfront commitment, dealer channel through Komatsu and Bell, tiered "AHS Editions".
- Built: kit rental or lease plus usage fee.
- Epiroc (ASI/LinkOA): OEM-agnostic conversion kits on project quotes.

Funding is bifurcating. Mega-rounds are going to generalists (Atoms $1.7B, Bedrock $270M at $1.75B, Applied Intuition $15B), while quarry-focused specialists stay small (sensmore $7.3M) or get acquired (SafeAI by Pronto, Teleo by Havoc, ASI Mining by Epiroc, Pronto by Atoms).

### Inferences
- **Pricing and channel:** dealer-channel retrofits (Pronto via Komatsu and Bell) set a low-touch benchmark. A new entrant probably needs either OEM or dealer distribution, or a clearly lower total cost of ownership. Pronto's camera-only tier is the direct price competitor.
- **Customer behaviour:** Heidelberg is deliberately multi-sourcing. Cemex and Lhoist work with sensmore. These large European aggregates firms are accessible as pilot customers, but they are already engaged with competitors.
- **Exit pattern:** acquisition (SafeAI, Teleo, ASI Mining, Pronto) shows consolidation is the typical outcome for retrofit specialists. The acquirers are OEMs (Epiroc) or well-funded platforms (Atoms, Havoc).

### Gaps
- No public per-truck price or subscription fee found for Pronto, SafeAI, sensmore, Epiroc/LinkOA, Hexagon, Teleo or Bedrock.
- No evidence found of Vulcan Materials or Martin Marietta autonomous haulage contracts.
- Kodiak's off-road (defence) program, Havoc's funding, and Hexagon's own driverless kit status were not researched in depth.

## 3. microagi and World Labs (Marble)

### Takeaway
- **microagi (Munich):** $55M seed in Jul 2026, led by Hummingbird. It claims this is Germany's largest seed. Valuation undisclosed. It is a data and deployment layer, not a robot maker.
- **World Labs:** $1B round in Feb 2026 at a reported ~$5B valuation. Marble went commercial in early 2026. AMD agreed on 2026-09-28 to acquire World Labs for ~$8.2B in stock; the deal is pending.

### Gaps

## 4. Typical retrofit sensor-kit BOM costs

### Takeaway
At list or reseller prices, a per-truck perception, compute and comms kit is about $15k–60k (EST). Lidar choice and redundancy drive most of the range. Industrial lidar runs from ~$750 (Livox Mid-360) to $8–18k (Ouster OS1-64/128); automotive lidar is ~$200 at OEM volume (Hesai ATX). Other components:
- Jetson AGX Orin 64GB devkit: $3,499 (after a 2025–26 price rise from $1,999).
- Septentrio dual-antenna RTK board: ~€610–700.
- Rugged GMSL2 IP67 cameras: ~$120–630 each.
- Rajant BreadCrumb mesh nodes: ~$2.4k–9.3k each.

No public prices were found for drive-by-wire actuation kits for haul trucks or for installation labour.

### Inferences
- **Illustrative per-truck BOM (EST, list or reseller prices):**

  | Item | Lean | Robust |
  |---|---|---|
  | Lidar | 2× Livox ~$2k | 2× Ouster OS1-64 ~$20k |
  | Cameras | 6× GMSL ~$1.5k | ~$3k |
  | Compute | AGX Orin ~$3.5k | IGX or dual Orin ~$8–15k |
  | GNSS-RTK | ~$1.5k incl. antennas | ~$3–5k |
  | On-truck Rajant node | ~$3.5k | ~$9k |
  | Enclosures, cabling, power | ~$3k | ~$5k |
  | **Total** | **~$15k** | **~$50–60k** |

  Excluded from these totals: drive-by-wire, e-stop/safety PLC, installation labour, and site infrastructure (mesh backbone, base station, control room).
- **Gross-margin implication:** hardware is a small share compared with OEM retrofit figures of $0.8–1.5M per truck. Engineering, safety certification and integration dominate cost, which supports a hardware-at-cost plus subscription model.
