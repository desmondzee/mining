# Measured benefits of autonomous haulage (AHS) and haul-truck safety statistics

Source-type labels used below: **[OPERATOR]** = reported by the mining company itself; **[VENDOR]** = OEM or tech vendor marketing or press release; **[REGULATOR]** = government data; **[SECONDARY]** = trade press or aggregator restating something; **[INDEPENDENT]** = academic or third party. Years are given wherever the source states them. Most hard operator numbers date from 2014–2020. Very few sources from 2021–2026 publish fresh percentages.

## 1. Productivity, utilisation and cost results from real AHS deployments

### Takeaway
Operator-reported results cluster around **15–20% lower load-and-haul cost**, **700–1,000 extra operating hours per truck per year**, and **~30% higher productivity**, all against manned fleets. They come mainly from large Pilbara iron-ore sites (Rio Tinto, BHP, Fortescue) and are mostly 2014–2020 figures. Vendor claims are similar or higher (Cat: +30% productivity, -20% cost; Komatsu: up to -15% load-and-haul unit cost). Quarry-scale deployments (Pronto at Heidelberg Materials) have only vendor "up to" figures (up to +20% productivity, 10–15% lifecycle cost savings) and no published before/after data.

### Cited Findings
**Rio Tinto (Pilbara iron ore), [OPERATOR], SEC filings**
- 2014: at Hope Downs 4, AHS beat the best manned Pilbara site on effective utilisation by **14%** and cut load-and-haul operating costs by **~13%** — [Rio Tinto 20-F FY2014](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000863064/000119312515079473/d820499dex152.htm)
- 2016: each of 72 autonomous trucks (about 20% of the fleet) ran **~1,000 more hours** at **15% lower cost** than comparable conventional trucks — [Rio Tinto 20-F FY2016](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000863064/000119312517067251/d284430dex152.htm)
- 2017: the autonomous fleet averaged **~700 more hours** at **15% lower load-and-haul cost** than comparable conventional trucks. Rio said higher utilisation of all trucks plus payload gains reduced fleet requirements, lowering capex and opex — [Rio Tinto 20-F FY2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000863064/000156459018004161/ex15.htm). The 2016 and 2017 sources give no reason for the drop from 1,000 to 700 hours.
- 2019: expected 50% of the truck fleet to be autonomous by end-2019, with more than 140 AHS trucks after the Marandoo and Brockman 4 retrofits. AutoHaul (autonomous rail) was fully operational — [Rio Tinto 6-K 2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/863064/000162828019009687/exhibits991.htm)
- Gap: no updated percentages from Rio Tinto for 2020–2026 were found.

**BHP (WAIO Jimblebar, and others), [OPERATOR]**
- Jimblebar is "our benchmark site for haulage costs", with a **20% reduction** since AHS was implemented, measured against other WAIO sites — [BHP 20-F FY2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/811809/000119312520250594/d914893d20f.htm); also [BHP 6-K 2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/811809/000119312519145956/d751248d6k.htm)
- A later BHP statement put autonomous fleet haulage costs **~20% below the WAIO average**, with **89% availability** — [International Mining, Oct 2022](https://im-mining.com/2022/10/03/bhp-opens-up-on-waios-autonomous-and-electric-fleet-future/) [SECONDARY quoting BHP]
- Jimblebar became BHP's first fully autonomous fleet in 2017 — [BHP, 10 years of Jimblebar, 2023](https://www.bhp.com/news/articles/2023/09/celebrating-10-years-of-jimblebar)
- Gap: no BHP figures for Goonyella (coal) were found.

**Fortescue (Solomon and Chichester hubs), [OPERATOR], ~2020–2021**
- The AHS fleet delivered a **>30% productivity increase** compared with previous manned fleets, after moving ~1,400 Mt (quoted by COO Greg Lilleyman) — [Australian Mining](https://www.australianmining.com.au/fortescue-reaches-autonomous-haulage-milestone/); [Mining Technology](https://www.mining-technology.com/news/fortescue-completes-chichester-automation-project/)
- Fleet of 183 trucks (Cat 793F, 789D, Komatsu 930E). They had travelled more than 52 million km and moved 1.5 Bt since 2013 — [E&MJ](https://www.e-mj.com/breaking-news/fortescue-completes-chichester-hub-ahs-project/)
- Fortescue release (2020): 100th autonomous truck conversion — [Fortescue](https://www.fortescue.com/en/articles/fortescue-automation-deployment-reaches-significant-milestone-2020-06-23)

**Caterpillar MineStar Command, [VENDOR]**
- Marketing claims: **+30% productivity, -20% operating cost, -50% safety incidents** — restated in [Mining Technology feature](https://www.mining-technology.com/features/autonomous-haulage-systems/) [SECONDARY]. Treat as unaudited vendor claims.
- Sept 2024: Command-equipped trucks had hauled **8.6 Bt**, with no reported injuries. A republished copy says 8.3 Bt, so the two versions conflict. The fleet is "hundreds of trucks" at dozens of sites on 3 continents — [Cat press release 2024](https://www.cat.com/en_US/news/machine-press-releases/cat-minestar-command-for-hauling-manages-the-autonomous-ecosystem.html)
- 2021: ~1.2 Bt hauled in a single year — [Cat release](https://www.cat.com/en_US/news/machine-press-releases/caterpillar-autonomously-hauls-more-than-1-billion-tonnes-of-material-with-cat-command-for-hauling-in-less-than-a-year.html). 2022: passed 5 Bt — [Cat release](https://www.cat.com/en_US/news/machine-press-releases/milestone-caterpillar-surpasses-5-billion-tonnes-of-material-autonomously-hauled.html)

**Komatsu FrontRunner, [VENDOR]**
- Load-and-haul total unit cost **reduced by up to 15%**, with a **13% reduction in total maintenance cost** — [International Mining, 2018](https://im-mining.com/2018/10/08/komatsu-mining-opens-automation-present-future-arizona-proving-grounds/)
- About 780 units deployed, having moved more than 10 Bt — [E&MJ](https://www.e-mj.com/features/minings-autonomous-ambitions/). Komatsu later commissioned its 1,000th AHS truck — [Skillings](https://www.skillings.net/autonomous-mining-komatsu-commissions-1000th-haul-truck/)

**Hitachi / Roy Hill (ASI Mining + Epiroc + Wenco), [OPERATOR]**
- Roy Hill said its AHS fleet was meeting safety and productivity targets and achieving "higher productivity rates than the conventional haul truck fleet". No percentage was given. Planned fleet: 54 Cat 793F, 24 Hitachi EH5000 and 18 EH4000 trucks — [E&MJ](https://www.e-mj.com/departments/suppliers-report/roy-hill-the-largest-autonomous-mine/); [Epiroc 2020](https://www.epirocgroup.com/en/media/corporate-press-releases/2020/20200212-epiroc-and-asi-mining-agree-with-australia-s-roy-hill-to-automate-its-mining-haul-truck-fleet)

**Suncor (oil sands), [OPERATOR]**
- All ore at Base Plant is moved by AHS (Komatsu 980E), in use since 2018. The fleet was ~120 trucks by July 2025, with 140 planned by end-2025. Benefits are claimed qualitatively (efficiency, safety, lower maintenance costs and downtime) with no percentages — [International Mining, Jul 2025](https://im-mining.com/2025/07/04/suncor-on-its-record-autonomous-ultraclass-truck-fleet/); [Suncor](https://www.suncor.com/en-ca/news-and-stories/our-stories/wins-on-our-autonomous-mining-journey)
- Mildred Lake: 19 Cat AHS trucks, with 21 more planned — [International Mining, Aug 2026](https://im-mining.com/2026/08/22/suncor-progresses-cat-ahs-at-mildred-lake-reports-success-with-mud-mode-on-komatsu-ahs-trucks-at-base-plant/)

**Teck, [OPERATOR]**
- Highland Valley Copper: 9 AHS trucks hauled ~26 Mt in 2019. By July 2020 the fleet had made 207,000 loads, driven 920,000 km and moved 47.7 Mt. A six-truck Komatsu 930E pilot ran at Elkview — [International Mining, Aug 2020](https://im-mining.com/2020/08/14/teck-accelerating-scaling-ahs-program-part-race21-innovation-driven-transformation/). No productivity or cost % was published.

**Boliden Aitik, [OPERATOR]**
- Bought Komatsu FrontRunner for 11 trucks for SEK 218m, with full implementation in 2024. The purchase was justified by productivity and utilisation, but no measured result has been published — [Boliden](https://investors.boliden.com/en/press/autonomous-hauling-system-increased-productivity-boliden-aitik-1956888)

**Quarries and aggregates: Pronto, SafeAI**
- Heidelberg Materials, Lake Bridgeport (TX): a Pronto autonomous mixed fleet (Cat and Komatsu trucks) moved more than 2 million tons pit-to-crusher in 8 months. It was described as North America's first fully autonomous mixed-fleet quarry. Operator motivation included difficulty recruiting drivers — [AggNet](https://www.agg-net.com/news/autonomous-haulage-milestone-for-heidelberg-materials); [Construction Owners](https://www.constructionowners.com/news/heidelberg-hits-2m-ton-milestone); [Equipment World](https://www.equipmentworld.com/technology/article/15826307/heidelberg-materials-pronto-partner-on-autonomous-dump-trucks) [OPERATOR/SECONDARY]
- Pronto CEO, describing customers: productivity **up to +20%**, fuel savings **>10%**, total lifecycle cost savings **10–15%** — [Mobility Engineering Tech / SAE](https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/52780-sae-ma-07554); [Equipment World, Komatsu–Pronto](https://www.equipmentworld.com/technology/article/15753104/komatsu-launches-autonomous-haul-truck-collaboration-with-pronto) [VENDOR]
- SafeAI and Obayashi: an electric and autonomous retrofit of a Cat 725 ADT. TCO is **projected** at 15–30% below the original diesel truck. This is a projection, not a field result — [GlobeNewswire, Dec 2023](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/12/12/2794774/0/en/SafeAI-and-Obayashi-Unveil-Electric-Autonomous-Construction-Haul-Truck.html) [VENDOR]

**Independent review**
- An MDPI review ("Automated Haulage Trucks: Impact on Workplace Safety and Efficiency in Surface Mining Systems") summarises ~20% haulage cost reductions and improved utilisation. It also notes Teck attributed production increases mainly to ore grade and recovery rather than AHS alone — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-6489/6/3/61). The full text could not be retrieved (HTTP 403); figures came via search snippet.

### Inferences
- A defensible central case from operator data is **~15% (range 13–20%) lower load-and-haul cost per tonne** and **~+700–1,000 hrs/truck/yr**. On a ~6,000–7,000 hr manned baseline that is roughly a 10–15% utilisation uplift. The productivity gain is **15–30%**, where the top of the range (Fortescue's >30%) includes fleet-wide changes.
- These are ultra-class iron-ore fleets with large, stable haul networks. Results for quarries (smaller trucks, shorter cycles, single shift) are unproven publicly. Quarry economics rest more on labour substitution, extending to 2nd and 3rd shifts, and driver shortages than on per-truck utilisation alone.
- Baselines are often "best manned site" or "WAIO average", so results are not controlled experiments.

### Gaps
- No public percentages after 2020 from Rio Tinto, BHP or Fortescue. Nothing found for BHP Goonyella, Hitachi's own AHS, Boliden results, or Teck cost results.
- No independently audited before/after data for any quarry deployment, including Pronto at Heidelberg.

## 2. Fuel and tyre savings

### Takeaway
Tyre and brake life improvements are the best-quantified secondary benefit: Komatsu claims ~40%. Fuel savings from autonomy are commonly claimed at ~10% but are mostly vendor statements or projections, not measured results.

### Cited Findings
- Komatsu: autonomous operation within the design envelope gives an **average 40% improvement in tyre and brake life** and a **13% reduction in overall maintenance** — [Komatsu AHS page (US)](https://www.komatsu.com/en-us/technology/smart-mining/loading-and-haulage/autonomous-haulage-system) [VENDOR]. Komatsu's Australian page attributes reduced tyre wear and lower fuel use to consistent speeds, gear shifts and braking, but gives no fuel % — [Komatsu AU](https://www.komatsu.com/en-au/technology/smart-solutions/smart-mining/autonomous-haulage-system)
- Pronto: fuel savings **>10%** at customer sites, per CEO — [SAE Mobility Engineering](https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/52780-sae-ma-07554) [VENDOR]
- Boliden Aitik: Ericsson projected up to **10% fuel savings** and ~9,400 t CO2e/yr for the truck fleet. This is a projection — [Ericsson blog 2018](https://www.ericsson.com/en/blog/2018/7/autonomous-mining-trucks-and-equipment--connected-pit-vipers-are-every-miners-dream) [VENDOR/projection]
- BHP reported improved truck availability (89%) on the autonomous fleet — [International Mining 2022](https://im-mining.com/2022/10/03/bhp-opens-up-on-waios-autonomous-and-electric-fleet-future/)
- Caution: Komatsu's often-quoted "5–15% fuel savings" relates to electronic controls on new conventional trucks, not autonomy — [Quarry Magazine](https://www.quarrymagazine.com/new-haul-trucks-enhanced-for-lower-emissions-fuel-savings/)

### Inferences
- Reasonable planning assumptions: **fuel -5 to -10%**, **tyre life +20 to +40%**, **maintenance -10 to -13%**. All are vendor-sourced, so a pitch should label them as vendor claims.

### Gaps
- No operator-published, audited fuel-per-tonne or tyre-cost comparison for AHS against manned trucks was found.

## 3. Safety: size of the problem and AHS safety outcomes

### Takeaway
Powered haulage is consistently the largest or second-largest cause of US mining deaths, at roughly **25–43% of fatalities** in recent years. Most of these deaths are at **surface** mines (8 of 10 in 2024 and in 2025 per MSHA's daily report). AHS operators report large drops in truck incidents: BHP reports >90% fewer significant truck events at Jimblebar and 75% fewer collisions. Caterpillar reports billions of tonnes hauled autonomously with no lost-time injuries.

### Cited Findings
**US (MSHA), [REGULATOR]**
- MSHA Daily Fatality Report (as of 16 Oct 2026): total mining fatalities were 30 (2022), 40 (2023), 28 (2024), 33 (2025), and 27 in 2026 year to date. Powered haulage fatalities (UG / surface) were 2/2 (2022), 3/7 (2023), 2/8 (2024), 2/8 (2025), and 2/6 in 2026 YTD — [MSHA Daily Fatality Report](https://arlweb.msha.gov/stats/charts/combined.php)
  - Implied powered haulage share: 2023 10/40 = 25%; 2024 10/28 = 36%; 2025 10/33 = 30%; 2026 YTD 8/27 = 30%.
- MSHA Powered Haulage page: in 2025, powered haulage caused **13 of 33 fatalities (39%)** and **426 of 4,792 nonfatal injuries (9%)** — [MSHA Powered Haulage Safety](https://www.msha.gov/safety-and-health/safety-and-health-initiatives/powered-haulage-safety). This **conflicts** with the daily report's count of 10 for 2025, probably because of classification differences or revisions. Cite both, or state the range.
- 2023: 10 of 40 deaths were powered haulage, and machinery plus powered haulage made up 65% — [Pit & Quarry](https://www.pitandquarry.com/mining-fatalities-totaled-40-in-2023/). An early-2025 snapshot put 2024 at 12 of 28 (43%) — [Pit & Quarry](https://www.pitandquarry.com/mine-fatalities-near-all-time-low-as-2024-approaches-finish/) [SECONDARY]
- 2021: 16 of 36 deaths were powered haulage, the highest rate since 2006 — [Safety+Health Magazine](https://safetyandhealthmagazine.com/articles/22065-msha-work-with-us-as-powered-haulage-other-concerns-persist); [Holmes Safety presentation](https://www.holmessafety.org/wp-content/uploads/12-Calhoun-Powered-Haulage-Initiative.pdf)
- MSHA engineers estimate adequate seat belts would save 3–4 lives per year. Surface mining vehicles can crush smaller vehicles the operator cannot see — [MSHA](https://www.msha.gov/safety-and-health/safety-and-health-initiatives/powered-haulage-safety)
- DOL (Dec 2023), on the Surface Mobile Equipment safety program rule: "powered haulage equipment and machinery have been the leading causes of serious and fatal mine accidents" — [DOL release](https://www.dol.gov/newsroom/releases/msha/msha20231219). The final rule's FRIA estimates 10-year benefits of $522M against costs of $111M (net $411M at 3%), annualised benefits of $61.3M — [MSHA FRIA](https://arlweb.msha.gov/REGS/REA/MSHA-1219-AB91-Safety-Program%20FRIA.pdf); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/12/20/2023-27640/safety-program-for-surface-mobile-equipment)
- March 2025: MSHA issued a safety alert after 10 fatalities early in the year — [Rock Products](https://rockproducts.com/2025/03/13/msha-issues-safety-alert-in-response-to-10-fatalities/)

**Australia, [REGULATOR]**
- All industries: vehicle incidents caused **41% (92) of worker fatalities in 2025**. In 2022, a vehicle was involved in 72% (141) of fatalities, and 41% of those involved a truck — [Safe Work Australia Key WHS Statistics 2026](https://data.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/reports/key-whs-statistics-australia/latest-release); [2024 edition](https://data.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/insights/key-whs-statistics-australia/2024)
- Mining: 7 fatalities (rate 2.4 per 100k) in 2022 — [SWA 2023 edition](https://data.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/insights/key-whs-statistics-australia/2023). A secondary report says 10 mining deaths in 2024 (3.4 per 100k) and needs checking against the PDF. Historically, 21 of 36 mining deaths (2007–08 to 2011–12) involved a vehicle — [SWA mining fact sheet](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/system/files/documents/1702/mining-fact-sheet-2011-12.pdf)
- NSW: a haul-truck operator died on 19 May 2025 when the truck went through a berm — [NSW Resources Regulator Q2 2025](https://www.resources.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/quarterly_safety_report_Apr-Jun2025.pdf)

**AHS safety outcomes, [OPERATOR] / [VENDOR]**
- BHP: significant events involving trucks at Jimblebar **dropped by more than 90%** after AHS was introduced — [BHP 20-F FY2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/811809/000119312520250594/d914893d20f.htm). Separately, BHP reported **75% fewer collision incidents** than at non-autonomous mines — [BHP 6-K 2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/811809/000119312520245468/d168177d6k.htm) (these are different metrics)
- Caterpillar: earlier releases claimed 3 Bt and then 5 Bt hauled with "zero lost-time injuries associated with automated truck operation" — [Cat 5 Bt release](https://www.cat.com/en_US/news/machine-press-releases/milestone-caterpillar-surpasses-5-billion-tonnes-of-material-autonomously-hauled.html). 2024: 8.6 Bt (or 8.3 Bt) with "no reported injuries" — [Cat 2024](https://www.cat.com/en_US/news/machine-press-releases/cat-minestar-command-for-hauling-manages-the-autonomous-ecosystem.html) [VENDOR]
- Fortescue CEO Elizabeth Gaines: AHS has "improved safety outcomes", not quantified — [Mining Technology](https://www.mining-technology.com/news/fortescue-completes-chichester-automation-project/)
- The MDPI review cites ~80% fewer heavy-vehicle safety incidents but also says mine-level safety metrics are rarely public, so treat the 80% as unverified — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-6489/6/3/61)

### Inferences
- Roughly **8 surface powered-haulage deaths per year** in the US (2023–2025). Removing drivers from haul trucks addresses the main exposure, though not pedestrians or light vehicles in mixed traffic.
- US metal/nonmetal surface mines, which include quarries, likely account for much of this. No breakdown by coal versus M/NM was retrieved.

### Gaps
- No haul-truck-only injury count for US quarries since 2019. This would need MSHA Mine Data Retrieval System queries.
- No current Australian national count of mining haul-truck deaths.

## 4. Published cost of an incident

### Takeaway
No operator-published cost of a specific haul-truck fatality was found. Societal benchmarks are available: NSC estimates **$1.54M per work death** (2024). MSHA's rule analysis puts powered-haulage-related prevention benefits at about $61M per year nationally.

### Cited Findings
- NSC (2024): **$1,540,000 cost per work-related death** and **$48,000 per medically consulted injury**. These are societal costs (wages, medical, administrative, employer costs) and exclude quality-of-life losses — [NSC Injury Facts](https://injuryfacts.nsc.org/work/costs/work-injury-costs/)
- MSHA Surface Mobile Equipment rule: $61.3M per year annualised benefit from reducing surface mobile equipment fatalities and injuries. The methodology is in Appendix A of the FRIA — [MSHA FRIA](https://arlweb.msha.gov/REGS/REA/MSHA-1219-AB91-Safety-Program%20FRIA.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a pitch, combine the NSC figure ($1.5M direct societal cost) with the regulatory value-of-statistical-life approach used in MSHA's FRIA. Indirect operator costs are not quantified here: production shutdowns after a fatality, MSHA citations and investigations, litigation, insurance.

### Gaps
- No published dollar figure for a specific mining-company haul-truck incident. The US DOT/DOL value-of-statistical-life figure used in the FRIA was not extracted. No Australian per-fatality cost for mining was found.
