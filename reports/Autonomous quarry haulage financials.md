# Retrofit autonomy pays back fastest on multi-shift quarries

A quarry retrofit autonomy kit has a real but narrow opening. The large-mine AHS market is owned by OEMs: Caterpillar, Komatsu and two Chinese OEMs supply **88% of the ~3,832 autonomous haul trucks** operating worldwide. The quarry class (40–100 t rigid trucks such as the HD465 and Cat 773–777) is almost entirely manned today, and the only proven player there, Pronto, was just acquired by Atoms. The numbers that matter in a pitch are labour numbers. A US quarry truck driver costs about **$80k a year fully loaded**, and a truck seat needs **1.1 operators on one shift, 2.1 on two shifts and 4.4–4.9 for 24/7**. On a mid-size truck the operator is about **25–35% of hourly operating cost**, compared with 12–15% on ultra-class mine trucks. Under our assumed pricing ($90k kit per truck, $75k site setup, $4,000 per truck per month), a 10-truck US quarry pays back in **~3.4 years on a single shift, ~1 year on two shifts and ~4 months at 24/7**. The product is therefore a shift-extension and labour-shortage play more than a cost-cutting play for single-shift sites. Operator-reported AHS results back the value claim: **13–20% lower load-and-haul cost** and **700–1,000 extra hours per truck per year** at Rio Tinto, BHP and Fortescue. Pricing is the weakest link. No retrofit vendor publishes a price, and our whole revenue model rests on one 2022 data point (Built Robotics at $3,000 per machine per month).

## The market is big on paper, but a retrofit startup must size it bottom-up

Headline AHS estimates disagree by almost an order of magnitude, because the bigger ones count the truck itself. MarketsandMarkets puts AHS at **$4.30B in 2025, growing 22% a year to $17.3B by 2032** ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/autonomous-mining-haulage-systems-market-266401441.html)). Marketintelo says $3.8B at 14.2% ([Marketintelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/autonomous-haulage-system-market)). Intel Market Research, which appears to count systems only, gives just **$543M growing 9.1%** ([Intel Market Research](https://www.intelmarketresearch.com/autonomous-haulage-systems-market-9613)). The broader "autonomous mining equipment" category is $3.7B (2024) at 9.9% CAGR ([Market.us](https://scoop.market.us/autonomous-mining-equipment-market-news/)). For a kit-plus-software business, the honest proxy is the **$0.5–0.9B systems-only figure**. No analyst sizes retrofit or quarry autonomy separately, so the deck should present a bottom-up number rather than the $17B headline.

Deployment is accelerating but concentrated. GlobalData counted **3,832 autonomous haul trucks in July 2025, up from 2,080 a year earlier**. China accounts for 2,090 of them and Australia for 1,024 ([Mining Technology / GlobalData](https://www.mining-technology.com/analyst-comment/china-global-leader-autonomous-surface-mining-trucks/)). Caterpillar had 827 trucks operating at end-2025 ([Caterpillar FY2025 Annual Report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000018230/000130817926000360/cat015318-ars.pdf)). Komatsu commissioned its 1,000th FrontRunner truck in April 2026 ([Construction Equipment Guide](https://www.constructionequipmentguide.com/komatsu-commissions-1000-ultra-class-autonomous-haul-trucks/71124?markdown=1)). Against a dated installed base of nearly **31,000 trucks of 100 t and above** ([Mining Weekly / Parker Bay](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/mining-equipment-markets-projected-to-remain-near-2015-lows-in-2016-2016-03-10)), autonomy covers roughly 10% or less of large trucks. Penetration of the sub-100 t quarry class is close to zero.

The quarry pool is large and fragmented. World aggregates output is **39 Bt a year** ([Aggregates Business / GAIN](https://aggbusiness.com/feature/global-aggregates-growth-examined-gain-convenor-jim-obrien)), produced at roughly 500,000 quarries and pits ([GAIN](https://www.aggbusiness.com/feature/growing-global-aggregates-sustainably)). The US produced **1.5B short tons of crushed stone worth $27B from ~3,500 quarries** ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-stone-crushed.pdf)), plus 870M tons of sand and gravel worth $12.6B from 6,500 pits ([USGS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-sand-gravel.pdf)). Europe has ~26,000 sites run by ~15,000 mostly small firms ([UEPG](https://uepg.eu/pages/facts)). No source publishes trucks per quarry. *Our assumption* is that 1,000–1,500 US crushed-stone quarries run 3–8 rigid trucks each, which gives **3,000–12,000 addressable US trucks**. At our pricing that is $145–575M of ARR (annual recurring revenue) potential plus $0.27–1.1B of kit revenue, before counting Europe, Australia or excavators.

The underserved claim holds, but the gap is closing. Caterpillar says its autonomy was built for large trucks running 24/7, and that it is only now "learning" to automate smaller fleets that don't run around the clock. Its 775 rollout to two more Luck Stone quarries was announced in September 2026 ([MarketScale / Equipment World](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/engineering-and-construction/cat-brings-driverless-hauling-on-the-smaller-775-truck-to-two-more-luck-stone-quarries)). Komatsu sells Pronto's technology for quarries instead of FrontRunner ([Pronto/Komatsu](https://pronto.ai/komatsu-north-america-and-pronto-launch-autonomy-solution-for-quarry-size-trucks/)), which suggests OEM mine AHS does not scale down economically. A 2024 simulation paper found **autonomous 40 t trucks beat manned 100 t trucks on NPV by ~31%** ([WSC 2024](https://informs-sim.org/wsc24papers/cea145.pdf)). That supports small-truck autonomy, though a related study was vendor-sponsored.

## Labour is a third of a quarry truck's hourly cost, so shift count drives everything

BLS data puts heavy truck drivers in nonmetallic mining and quarrying at **$26.96/h mean ($56,070/yr)**, against $35.67/h in metal ore mining ([BLS OEWS May 2025](https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v1/timeseries/data/OEUN000000021230053303204); [metal ore](https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v1/timeseries/data/OEUN000000021220053303204)). Benefits are **30.0% of total employer compensation** ([BLS ECEC](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.nr0.htm)), so a quarry driver costs about **$38.5/h, or ~$80k/yr loaded**. Australian ads run **A$51–56/h in the Bowen Basin** ([WorkPac](https://www.workpac.com/job/dump-truck-operator-298)) and up to A$63–65/h in SA ([Randstad](https://www.randstad.com.au/jobs/dump-truck-operator_sandalwood_60107b4c-a34a-4e07-910e-3a5965f3468b/)), with a Seek midpoint near A$113k ([WhatsTheSalary](https://www.whatsthesalary.com/salaries/au/mining-resources-and-energy/mining-operations)). UK mobile machine operators earn a median of ~£30.5–32.4k ([UCAS SOC 8229](https://www.ucas.com/explore/career-page/3.1/8229)). German tipper drivers earn ~€3,488/month ([gehalt.de](https://www.gehalt.de/beruf/kipperfahrer)). Roster arithmetic (8,760 h ÷ ~1,800–2,000 worked hours per FTE) gives the per-truck staffing below. It matches the "4–5 operators per truck for 24/7" figure in industry commentary.

| Setting | Operators per truck | Labour cost per truck per year |
|---|---|---|
| US quarry, one shift | 1.1 | ~$90k |
| US quarry, two shifts | 2.1 | ~$170k |
| US metal mine, 24/7 | 4.4–4.9 | ~$470–520k |
| Australian mine, 24/7 | 4.4–4.9 | ~US$0.6–0.7M |
| UK quarry, two shifts | 2.1 | ~£84k |

*Derived from the BLS, job-ad and aggregator sources above; Australian on-costs are our estimate.*

Fuel is the best-sourced non-labour line. Caterpillar telematics give medium-load burn of **9.9 gal/h (773G), 10.7 (775G), 15.6 (777G) and 23.3 (785)** ([Cat Performance Handbook 2022](https://wheelercat.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Cat-Performance-Handbook-from-VST-fuel-consumption-2022-12-09T21-20-09.pdf)). A Komatsu HD465 measured 51.9 L/h on hard overburden ([Indonesian Mining Journal](https://jurnal.tekmira.esdm.go.id/index.php/imj/article/view/1578)). At ~$3.50/gal diesel ([EIA via Rigzone](https://www.rigzone.com/news/eia_sees_gasoline_diesel_price_dropping_in_2025_2026-02-dec-2025-182432-article/)), and with tyres and maintenance estimated from vendor cost shares ([1MERCA](https://onemerca.com/blog/cat-777-haul-truck-operating-cost-per-hour-ghana-gold-mining/)), we estimate total operating cost below. The key point is that **the operator share falls as trucks get bigger**. Autonomy therefore saves a larger fraction of cost on quarry trucks than on the ultra-class trucks OEMs built it for.

| Cost per operating hour (US, est.) | 55–65 t (773/775, HD465) | 90–100 t (777) | 140 t (785) / 230 t (793) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fuel | $35–38 | $55 | $82 / $147 |
| Tyres + maintenance | $40–70 | $70–110 | — / $130–240 |
| Operator (loaded) | ~$38 | $38–51 | ~$51 |
| **Total** | **$115–145** | **$165–215** | — / **$330–440** |
| **Operator share** | **25–35%** | **20–30%** | **12–15%** (793) |

Load and haul is about **22% of total cost** in an old US quarry costing model ([911Metallurgist](https://911metallurgist.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/The-Silo-Costing-Approach.pdf)). Bell Equipment puts it at 25–30% (unverified). At mines it is **about two-thirds**, for example at Kumba's Sishen ([BusinessLive](https://www.businesslive.co.za/bd/companies/mining/2018-05-29-workers-remain-the-backbone-at-kumba/)). Labour scarcity supports the case: **77% of US construction firms struggle to hire heavy equipment operators** ([AGC 2025](https://www.agc.org/sites/default/files/users/user21902/2025_Workforce_Survey_National_FINAL_m.pdf)), and seven Australian machinery-operator occupations have been in persistent shortage since 2021 ([JSA 2025 OSL](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-10/2025%20OSL%20Key%20Findings%20Report.pdf)).

## Operators report 15–20% haulage savings and >90% fewer truck incidents

The strongest evidence comes from SEC filings, not vendor marketing. Rio Tinto reported that its autonomous trucks each ran **~1,000 more hours at 15% lower cost** in 2016 ([Rio Tinto 20-F FY2016](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000863064/000119312517067251/d284430dex152.htm)), and **~700 more hours at 15% lower load-and-haul cost** in 2017 ([20-F FY2017](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000863064/000156459018004161/ex15.htm)). BHP says Jimblebar haulage costs fell **20%** ([BHP 20-F FY2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/811809/000119312520250594/d914893d20f.htm)). Fortescue reported **>30% productivity** over its manned fleets ([Australian Mining](https://www.australianmining.com.au/fortescue-reaches-autonomous-haulage-milestone/)). Vendor claims are in the same range: Komatsu claims up to −15% load-and-haul cost, **+40% tyre and brake life** and −13% maintenance ([Komatsu](https://www.komatsu.com/en-us/technology/smart-mining/loading-and-haulage/autonomous-haulage-system)). Pronto claims up to +20% productivity and >10% fuel savings at customer sites ([SAE Mobility Engineering](https://www.mobilityengineeringtech.com/component/content/article/52780-sae-ma-07554)). The caveat a judge will raise is that all the hard numbers come from 2014–2020 ultra-class iron-ore fleets. **No audited before/after quarry data exists**. The best quarry proof point is Pronto's mixed Cat/Komatsu fleet at Heidelberg's Lake Bridgeport, which hauled **2M+ tons in 8 months** ([AggNet](https://www.agg-net.com/news/autonomous-haulage-milestone-for-heidelberg-materials)).

Safety is the second argument. MSHA counts **13 of 33 US mining deaths in 2025 (39%) as powered haulage** ([MSHA](https://www.msha.gov/safety-and-health/safety-and-health-initiatives/powered-haulage-safety)). Its daily fatality report gives a lower 10 of 33, with about 8 a year at surface mines ([MSHA daily report](https://arlweb.msha.gov/stats/charts/combined.php)), so quote the range of 25–43%. After AHS, BHP saw significant truck events at Jimblebar **drop by more than 90%** ([BHP 20-F FY2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/811809/000119312520250594/d914893d20f.htm)) and **75% fewer collisions** than non-autonomous sites ([BHP 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/811809/000119312520245468/d168177d6k.htm)). The NSC values a work death at **$1.54M** in societal cost ([NSC](https://injuryfacts.nsc.org/work/costs/work-injury-costs/)). Our model excludes safety savings and the productivity uplift, so both are upside.

## A $90k kit undercuts OEM AHS by 10–25×, but Pronto/Atoms sets the price

The only public OEM anchor is Boliden Aitik: **SEK 218M (~US$25M) for Komatsu AHS on 11 ultra-class trucks**, roughly $2.3M per truck all-in ([International Mining](https://im-mining.com/2021/10/22/boliden-to-become-eu-automation-frontrunner-with-help-of-komatsu-ahs/)). A vendor-sponsored Whittle model assumes $1.5M to adapt each truck, and **4 staff per shift for the whole fleet** in place of one operator per truck ([Whittle Consulting](https://www.whittleconsulting.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Autonomous-Haulage-Study-Report-Rev-F.pdf)). An unsourced 2026 guide cites $0.8–1.5M per ultra-class retrofit and $3–15M of site infrastructure ([DirtMatch](https://dirtmatch.com/blog/autonomous-semi-autonomous-haul-trucks-guide-2026)). At list prices, our sensor pod costs a fraction of that. Lidar ranges from a ~$749 Livox Mid-360 ([Lidarseek](https://lidarseek.com/product/livox-mid360)) to ~$8–12k for an Ouster OS1-64 ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/305902230376)). ## The model: $975k upfront, $480k a year, payback driven by shifts

**Assumptions (ours, not sourced).** Kit price is **$90k per truck**: about 10–25× below OEM AHS, with ~$55k COGS covering the sensor pod, drive-by-wire and install. Site setup is **$75k**: drone scan, simulation build, mesh backbone and commissioning, with ~$40k COGS. The subscription is **$4,000 per truck per month**, anchored on Built's 2022 $3,000 per month plus inflation and haul-truck complexity, at an assumed 70% gross margin. After automation, the site keeps **2 staff per shift** (a supervisor and a technician) at 1.25× operator cost. Voice dispatch is assumed to remove the 4-person control room in the Whittle model. Non-labour savings are a conservative **8% of ~$95/h** truck running cost, below vendor claims for fuel, tyres and maintenance. Productivity uplift, safety and insurance savings are **excluded**. The fleet is 10 trucks in the 55–65 t class, using the hours and operator ratios from the labour section.

| 10-truck quarry, US ($80k/operator) | One shift (1,400 h) | Two shifts (2,600 h) | 24/7 (5,800 h) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operator cost removed | $880k | $1,680k | $3,760k |
| Retained supervisor/tech cost | −$220k | −$420k | −$940k |
| Fuel/tyre/maintenance saving (8%) | $106k | $198k | $441k |
| Subscription (10 × $48k) | −$480k | −$480k | −$480k |
| **Net annual benefit to quarry** | **$286k** | **$978k** | **$2,781k** |
| Upfront (10 × $90k + $75k) | $975k | $975k | $975k |
| **Payback** | **3.4 yr** | **1.0 yr** | **0.35 yr (~4 months)** |

**Sensitivity by region** (same model, loaded operator cost varied). In **Australia (~$105k)**, payback is 2.0 / 0.7 / 0.3 years. In the **UK (~$52k)**, it is **17.6 years on one shift**, 1.8 years on two shifts and 0.5 years at 24/7. Because the subscription is flat, single-shift quarries in low-wage markets don't pencil out. The fix is usage-based pricing (per autonomous hour, the model Built and Carbon Robotics use), or targeting multi-shift sites and quarries that want to add an unmanned second shift. Excavator retrofits are not modelled. They are an upsell, with surface excavator operators earning $57k in quarries ([BLS](https://api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v1/timeseries/data/OEUN000000021230047502204)).

## Risks a judge will probe

Competition and consolidation are the first risk. Pronto, now backed by Atoms' $1.7B, holds the flagship quarry customer and the Komatsu, Bell and Hitachi dealer channels. Every retrofit specialist before it has ended up acquired: SafeAI, Teleo, ASI and Pronto itself ([Plant & Equipment](https://www.plantandequipment.com/news/mining/atoms-acquires-pronto-to-expand-autonomous-mining-technology)). That makes acquisition the likely exit and a well-funded rival the likely obstacle. OEMs are also moving down-market, with Caterpillar commercialising 775/777 quarry autonomy through dealers, and they control the truck CAN interfaces that drive-by-wire depends on. The simulation pipeline carries a platform dependency. **AMD agreed on 28 September 2026 to buy World Labs for ~$8.2B in stock**, pending close ([Northeast Times / CNBC](https://northeasttimes.com/2026/09/28/amd-to-buy-ai-research-lab-world-labs-in-8-2-billion-stock-deal)), so Marble's access and pricing may shift toward AMD hardware. That would clash with a Jetson/NVIDIA edge stack, and the pitch should show a fallback route from drone scan to simulation. Pricing evidence is thin: **no retrofit vendor publishes a kit or subscription price**, and the only quarry benefit data is vendor "up to" claims. Our $90k + $4k/month should be presented as a hypothesis to validate in pilot LOIs (letters of intent). Finally, the safety case (functional-safety certification, MSHA acceptance) is the real cost driver behind OEM pricing, and our BOM excludes it.

## Conclusion

The financial story that survives scrutiny is about labour per shift, not "AI saves 15%". A quarry truck's operator is a third of its hourly cost, and every added shift multiplies the operators needed while the kit cost stays fixed. That is why payback falls from about three years to about four months as a site moves from one shift to 24/7. The pitch should target multi-shift, labour-constrained, mixed-fleet independents in the US and Australia, and propose usage-based pricing for single-shift sites. Pronto/Atoms is the price setter.
